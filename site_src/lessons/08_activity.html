Every animal is now a track with a consistent ID. From these tracks we compute distance travelled, speed, behavior state and an activity index. The key points are the perspective correction that converts pixels to meters, and making sure bounding-box jitter is not mistaken for real movement.

1. Pixels → meters: perspective correction based on body height

The perspective model h(y) from the previous lesson answers “how many pixels tall is one animal at screen position y?”. If we take the height of an adult Hanwoo (withers plus head) to be about 1.3 m:

m_per_px(y) = 1.3 / h(y)
distance[m] ≈ pixel displacement × m_per_px(y)
This is an approximation that needs no separate calibration. If you know the real-world coordinates of four points on the floor (e.g. the pen corners), you can convert more accurately with a homography (Lesson 11).

2. Jitter removal and speed

  • Two-stage smoothing of the bbox center: 1-second moving median → moving average (the median removes spikes first)
  • Speed = ±0.5-second central difference of the smoothed position (frame-to-frame differences are very sensitive to jitter)
  • Speed cap of 2 m/s: suppresses fake speeds that appear when a box suddenly grows or shrinks due to occlusion
  • Speeds below 0.05 m/s are excluded from the cumulative distance (so the jitter of a standing animal does not add up to distance)

3. Behavior state classification

StateConditionMeaning
🟢 Walkspeed ≥ 0.20 m/sMoving on foot
🟠 Active (local activity)speed ≥ 0.05 m/s or motion energy ≥ 2.5Feeding in place, head/body movement, slowly shifting position
⚪ RestotherwiseStanding or lying at rest

Motion energy = mean brightness difference between consecutive frames inside the bbox. It captures in-place movement, such as lowering the head to eat feed while the position stays the same. In real data, stationary animals scored about 0.3–1 and feeding or moving animals about 2–6, so the threshold was set to 2.5.

4. Activity index (0–100)

activity index = 100 × ( 0.7 × min(speed/0.5, 1) + 0.3 × min(motion/5, 1) )

A single metric that combines locomotion (70%) and in-place movement (30%). It is the basis for comparing animals, tracking trends over time, and detecting abnormal animals (a sharp drop in activity → possible illness; a sharp rise → possible estrus).
