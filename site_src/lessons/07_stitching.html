If a ReID model is available (models/hanwoo_reid.pt), only the first stitching pass from this lesson is used, and long-term reappearances are handled by the fixed-herd ID assignment in the next lesson. Without a ReID model, the two-pass stitching described here runs as-is.

An online tracker must decide using only the information it has “so far”. When analyzing recorded video, however, we can decide again after seeing the entire video. A barn offers strong constraints — an enclosed space + a fixed camera + the same herd — so re-joining broken pieces (tracklets) is the most effective way to improve ID consistency.

1. Algorithm

  1. Noise removal: tracklets shorter than 0.5 s (12 frames) are treated as false detections and removed
  2. Perspective model: the same animal looks smaller near the top of the frame (farther away). Fit the relation between bbox height h and center y over all detections, h(y) = a·y + b → normalize distances into “body-size units”
  3. Link candidates: tracklet j starts after tracklet i ends (no time overlap), gap ≤ 20 s, and the end-to-start distance ≤ 2.5 + 0.6×gap(s) body lengths
  4. Cost = 0.5 × (distance / allowed distance) + 0.5 × appearance distance (Bhattacharyya) + 0.15 × (gap / max gap)
  5. Hungarian for optimal 1:1 ‘end’ ↔ ‘start’ linking; only pairs with cost < 0.85 are merged. Repeat until no more links are found (handles chains i→j→k)
  6. Two-pass stitching: the process above is run twice with different conditions

    Max gap20 s5 min

  7. Reassign IDs (1, 2, 3 … in order of appearance) + linear interpolation only for momentary missed detections of 0.5 s or less (drawn as thin boxes in the output video)
Keep interpolation short! At first we interpolated gaps up to 20 s, and while cattle lay down and their detections dropped out, virtual boxes and movement paths were drawn where no animal was; that fake movement made up 23–32% of the total distance travelled. Now we fill only gaps of 0.5 s or less and leave longer gaps as “not visible”. Activity is also computed only from frames with actual detections (Lesson 09).

Why use bbox height for perspective?

Cattle are nearly the same real size, so changes in on-screen size come mostly from the distance to the camera. Many boxes are truncated by occlusion, which biases a simple mean downward, so we fit robustly using the 75th-percentile height per y-band (mostly unoccluded cattle).

Appearance feature: H-S color histogram

A 2D histogram of hue (H) and saturation (S) in HSV over the central 60% of the bbox (minimizing fence and background). Leaving out value (V) makes it insensitive to shade and lighting changes, and we use the average over the whole tracklet. Hanwoo are similar in color, but differences in coat shade and white markings are captured, and combined with the distance condition this gives enough discriminative power.
